## 1. Cutting post-Gen-3 Pokémon

### What's measured
pokeemerald-expansion has a switch per generation (`P_GEN_4_POKEMON` … `P_GEN_9_POKEMON` in `include/config/species_enabled.h`) and one per family. Families are filed under their **oldest** member's generation, and cross-generation evolutions (Magnezone, Weavile, Togekiss, Munchlax, Ursaluna…) stay on through `P_CROSS_GENERATION_EVOS`. So turning gens 4–9 off is exactly "keep Gen 1–3 and their evolutions". Regional forms of Gen 1–3 species (Alolan Vulpix, Hisuian Growlithe, Bloodmoon Ursaluna) also stay.

| Build (release) | ROM used | Free | Saved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Today (`hoenn` @ #93) | 32,711,444 | 823 KB | — |
| Gens 4–9 off, **keep list below** (recommended) | 24,136,436 | 9,197 KB | 8,575,008 B (8.2 MB) |
| Gens 4–9 off, nothing kept | 23,774,564 | 9,550 KB | 8,936,880 B (8.5 MB) |

Both builds compiled and linked with no other change (not booted in the emulator yet). The savings are graphics, cries, learnsets, dex text and icons of ~975 species entries; the species *numbers* stay, so saves and tables don't shift.

### Why it's nearly free
**HnS barely uses those species already.** A scan of every map, script, wild table and trainer party in the HnS build found Gen 4+ families only in:
- the **Sinjoh Ruins** story (Arceus, plus the Regigigas, Regieleki and Regidrago rooms; Dialga/Palkia/Giratina through Arceus's event),
- the **Alola islands** (the four Tapus; Noibat and Noivern on Ula'ula, 2 trainer slots),
- our own Melemele house (a Rockruff and a Pikipek, part 11).

No HnS wild table and only those two trainers use any other Gen 4+ family. Part 33's living dex tool already treats the 77 Sinnoh-only species as out of scope (Bryan, Nov 27). So the cut matches what the game already is.

**Keep list** (measured above): Arceus, Dialga, Palkia, Giratina, Regigigas, Regieleki, Regidrago, the four Tapus, Noibat, Rockruff, Pikipek. Rockruff and Pikipek could be swapped for Gen 1–3 pets instead (saves a little more).

### What it costs
- **The randomizer's "all generations" option** (HnS challenge menu, GEN SCOPE row) would only have Gen 1–3 to draw from. Its default is already Gen 1–3.
- **Link trades with unmodified HnS** could bring in a species that has no data here. HnS doesn't support trading with other hacks anyway, but worth one line in the release notes.
- The Pokédex and the PC need a check that disabled entries are skipped (the HGSS dex code already calls `IsSpeciesEnabled`).
- Part 33's living dex tool should read the same switch, so it never asks for a disabled species.

### Recommendation (updated after Bryan's answer)
**Held in reserve (Bryan: not now).** 2.1's own additions (custom sprites, wallpapers, text, a few maps) should fit in what's left after the lanes, roughly 150–250 KB. **Trigger:** if the release build drops under ~300 KB free, the cut becomes the next part (M: config + keep list + one emulator pass through dex, PC, randomizer, Battle Frontier and trade). Bridgette's team (section 10) adds Garchomp's, Lucario's and Rotom's families to the keep list.

## 2. Berry spots in Hoenn

What the repo shows (static check; not run in the emulator):
- **88 Hoenn berry trees** on kept maps, all with their own tree IDs. Part 19 already moved the three that collided with HnS trees; a fresh scan finds **no shared IDs** left (123 trees in all, 35 HnS).
- **All are planted** by `EventScript_ResetAllBerries` at new game. The 8 soil patches on Routes 104, 118, 121 and 123 start empty, as in Emerald.
- **Growth runs everywhere**: HnS's clock calls `BerryTreeTimeUpdate` for every tree, Hoenn's included (Gen 3 growth and yield rates).
- **Watering:** the berry tree script only accepts the **Wailmer Pail**, which only the Route 104 flower shop gives (a Hoenn flag, so it works). HnS's Squirt Bottle is just the Sudowoodo item and can't water. Watering only raises yield, so Johto trees still grow; they just give fewer berries before Hoenn.

**Recommendation:** small (S), fold into the swarm part (41):
1. Emulator check: pick a Hoenn tree, plant in a soil patch, water, advance the clock, harvest.
2. Let the Squirt Bottle water too (one `checkitem` line), so Johto players can water before Hoenn. Optional.

## 3. Swarms

### What happened
- **HnS has no HGSS swarm system** (no daily swarm, no Mom/radio announcement). The only swarm code is Emerald's **TV mass outbreak** (`src/tv.c`).
- It runs in HnS: once `FLAG_SYS_GAME_CLEAR` is set (HnS's champion), each `TryPutPokemonTodayOnAir` call has a 1-in-200 chance to start an outbreak show; watching it starts the outbreak for a day.
- **It points at the wrong maps.** It stores map group 0 + Emerald's map number. In the HnS build, group 0 is HnS's towns and routes, so the five Emerald outbreaks land on Johto/Kanto maps (found by reading the code; not emulator-checked):

| Emerald outbreak | Lands on (HnS group 0, same number) |
|---|---|
| Seedot Lv 3, Route 102 | Route 35 |
| Nuzleaf Lv 15, Route 114 | Route 47 |
| Seedot Lv 13, Route 117 | Viridian City |
| Seedot Lv 25, Route 120 | Vermilion City |
| Skitty Lv 8, Route 116 | Pallet Town |

  The show names the map from its header, so it would really say "Route 35". This is upstream HnS behavior, not something the Hoenn addition caused, but Hoenn's Lv 35–70 bands make Lv 3 swarms look even stranger.

### Recommendation: rework (2.1-07, M)
- Replace Emerald's five-entry list with a **Hoenn & Seek swarm table**: map (group + number, any region), species, level from the area's band, a rate. One swarm a day, rolled with the daily reset (HGSS style), **announced** on the TV news (part 13's hook), as a segment on the PokéGear radio (section 16's segment table) and on the Seeker's Journal's Today page (section 15).
- **Swarm-only species: yes, a few.** Good candidates are species the game has data for but no source: **Galarian forms of Gen 1–3** (Ponyta, Slowpoke, Farfetch'd, Meowth, Mr. Mime, Corsola, Zigzagoon, Weezing, the Galar birds are better as events), and some Alolan/Hisuian forms not already on the islands. They stay in the build after the cut (they're Gen 1–3 species). A daily rotation means every one comes up within a couple of weeks; the living dex tool would count the swarm table as a source.
- Also a few HGSS-flavored swarms of normal species (Yanma, Dunsparce, Qwilfish, Snubbull, Marill in Johto; Hoenn ones like Skitty, Seedot, Spinda at its band level).
- **Part 33 (2.0, lane B) owns distribution.** The swarm table must go through its living dex tool (#102); by 2.1 it's merged.

## 4. The rival (Silver)

### Where he is today
HnS's Silver has 7 teams per starter. The late ones: **Victory Road** (Lv 45–48), **Mt. Moon** (Lv 64) and a **daily rematch at the Indigo Plateau Pokémon Center** (fixed Lv 68: Bloodmoon Ursaluna, Crobat, Victreebel/Octillery/…, Houndoom, his starter, Tyranitar; 31 IVs, Basic AI). The player's cap passes 68 at the first Kanto badges and reaches 84–85 in Hoenn, so Silver stops mattering halfway through.

### Proposal (2.1-08, M; 2.1-09, L)
**Scaling (2.1-08).** Built on the shared scaled-opponent ladder (section 16), which Silver, the friend trainers and the second rival all use. Use the same ladder as DAMIAN (part 16): his ace = the player's **progress cap**, worked out as if LEVEL CAP were on (`HoennGetPostJohtoLevelCap(…, FALSE)`), whatever the challenge setting. Cap it at **Brendan & May's level (ace 87)**. Gaps inside the team stay. Applies to the Indigo rematch (and Mt. Moon if you meet him late).

**Other ways his team gets stronger**, unlocked at milestones (Kanto champion, 4 Hoenn badges, Wallace, Brendan & May), each a new team tier:
1. Held items on every Pokémon (Life Orb, Choice Scarf, Leftovers, a berry), not just Full Restores.
2. Real movesets: coverage and setup moves instead of HnS's mid-game sets.
3. EVs (HnS's Red-style spreads) on top of his 31 IVs.
4. **Smart AI** from the second tier on (as on Red).
5. Roster growth that fits HGSS: Sneasel → Weavile, Magnemite → Magnezone, Gastly line, and one **Hoenn catch** after you've been there (he followed you; e.g. Bagon line or Beldum line).
6. One Hoenn appearance (e.g. at the Ever Grande Pokémon League after you win), so he's in the new region too, plus a PokéGear-free line from Brendan or May who ran into him.

**His starter following him (2.1-09). Decided (Bryan, round 4): both halves**: it follows him during his cutscenes (the C helper below) and stands next to him wherever you can move around him. Two ways were considered for the cutscenes:
- Duplicate every `applymovement` in his scenes for a second object (simple but ~15 scenes × 2 movement lists, easy to get out of step).
- **Recommended:** a small C helper: while a scene runs, the starter object steps onto the tile Silver just left (follower-style), so existing movement scripts stay untouched. The object's sprite is set per scene from `VAR_STARTER_MON` and his current stage (Chikorita → Bayleef → Meganium etc.).
- Where he stands still (Indigo, Mt. Moon), the starter stands beside or behind him like the gym followers (part 4b).
- Cost: a new C file + one hook line per scene (~15 HnS scenes). Large sprites are fine for NPC objects; HnS's 32×32 indoor rule only applies to the player's follower.

**Lane conflicts:** none; 2.1 starts after the lanes finish.

## 6. Gym leaders' weekends and fan-favorite events

### Weekends off
**Decided (Bryan):** a leader takes weekends off **once you've beaten them**, so nobody is ever blocked from a first challenge. On Saturdays and Sundays they and their follower are hidden in the gym (the gym guide says where they went) and appear at a weekend spot in the world with a line and their usual rematch.
- 24 leaders across Johto, Kanto and Hoenn; each needs a spot, a hide/show call (`GetDayOfWeek`, part 2) and a few lines. Hint lines say who's where.
- Size: one part per region (M each, see "2.1 order"). Uses part 4b's followers (they share the leader's flag, so they move together).
- **Calendar, not progress (Bryan, round 4).** Changing the day at a wall clock skips the wait, and that's fine: it's the Animal Crossing model. Same for the chains below and Dan's weekly episodes.

### Fan-favorite chains (multi-week, fan service)
HnS already has HGSS's cameos to build on: **Misty** on Route 25 (the date scene), **Brock** at Diglett's Cave, **Blue** on Cinnabar. Proposal: one chain each for **Brock, Misty and Blue**, a step per weekend over 3–4 weeks (weekly flags from part 2), each step a short scene somewhere fitting (Brock: Mt. Moon fossils, Pewter Museum, a breeding question at the Day Care; Misty: Cerulean Cape, Seafoam's currents, a water show; Blue: Viridian Gym, Daisy and Oak in Pallet, a sparring match at Cinnabar). Good additions if you want more: **Lance** (Dragon's Den), **Whitney** (Goldenrod), **Steven** (Hoenn).
- **Reward: unique secret base decorations.** Dolls are cheap (they use follower sprites; IDs 127–255 are free, 129 slots): e.g. Onix, Starmie, Pidgeot dolls. A unique poster or mat per character needs new art, which costs a sitting each.
- Size: one part per character (M each). Weekly flags come from 2.1's own flag block (section 16): 2.0 leaves only 3 weekly flags spare.

### Deeper conversations
Feasible and cheap (text only, ~1–2 KB per character). The hint engine (part 7) already picks a line by conditions on flags and vars. Each fan favorite gets a pool of 15–25 lines drawn from the games and the show, unlocked by what you've done (Misty notices your Psyduck following you; Brock asks about your Hoenn fossils; Blue knows you beat Red). Fold each pool into that character's chain part.

## 8. More Pokémon in towns and buildings

HnS already has **1,561 Pokémon objects** on its own maps, but almost all sit in scenery, and only **66 of 241 HnS interiors** have one. Proposal: a generator pass that gives people their Pokémon:
- **Owner–pet pairs** in towns: next to an NPC, on a walkable tile, facing them or wandering one tile; species fits the person (fisherman → Poliwag, old lady → Meowth, kid → Pichu) or the local wild table.
- **Houses and shops:** one pet per interior that has none (running the cry script like part 8's house pets).
- Rules: never block a path or door (the reachability flood fill from part 20a), and pass the screen checker below.
- Size: generator + Johto/Kanto (M), Hoenn (S).

### Checking objects on screens (Bryan, round 4: "the best way")
Every 2.1 part that adds objects (town pets, gym redesign, friend trainers, chains, Silver's starter, swarm objects) runs one shared checker instead of each part counting by hand. Today the rules are spread out: the overworld generator's ≤ 9 per screen (11 in towns), part 4b's palette-slot pass in the emulator, the reachability flood fill.
- **`tools/hoenn/check_screens.py`** (2.1-03, S): for every map, slides a GBA screen window (15×10 tiles, plus the one-tile margin objects spawn in) over every position the player can stand on, and counts what can be on screen at once: object events (with their flags' day/night/weekday exclusions, so a day mon and a night mon on one tile count once), the player, the player's follower and the slots field effects and weather need.
- **Two budgets:** objects (the engine's 16 object events, minus a margin) and **sprite palettes** (16 OBJ slots: count distinct palette tags, since Pokémon objects each bring their own via dynamic palettes). The reserved slots are measured once in the emulator (player, follower, weather, field effects, the worst case) and written into the tool, so later parts don't re-measure.
- **Run by the generators** (`gen_hoenn_ow_mons.py`, followers, buildings, hints) and by `regen_all.sh --check`, so an over-budget placement fails the build instead of glitching in the playtest. The emulator check (part 4b's recipe) is only for the worst screen the tool reports per part.

## 13. Quality of life: boxes, wallpapers, bag

### PC boxes: 14 boxes × 30 = 420 slots (deeper look, Bryan's question 6)

**How the save is laid out.** The save chip is 128 KB flash, the most a GBA cartridge save can be, split into 32 sectors of 4 KB. Each sector holds 3,968 B of normal data plus a 116 B slice of SaveBlock3. Sectors 0–27 are **two copies of the save** (14 sectors each, written alternately so a power cut mid-save never loses everything); 28–31 are the Hall of Fame (2), Trainer Hill and the Frontier's recorded battle.

**Free space today, measured with the real compiler (`sizeof` in the HnS build):**

| Block | Sectors | Room | Used | Free |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SaveBlock2 (player, options, Frontier) | 1 | 3,968 | 3,892 | 76 |
| SaveBlock1 (world: bag, flags, bases, TV…) | 4 | 15,872 | 15,760 | 112 |
| PC storage (`struct PokemonStorage`) | 9 | 35,712 | 34,144 | 1,568 |
| SaveBlock3 (Hoenn & Seek's flags/vars) | slices of 14 | 1,624 | 948 | 676 |
| **Total free per save copy** | | | | **2,432** |

So yes, there's a little room: 2,432 B. One box costs **2,410 B** (30 Pokémon × 80 B, plus its name and wallpaper byte). But that room is in four separate pieces and a box has to be one block, so as it stands it adds 19 slots at most (the PC's own 1,568 B), not a box.

**Where more room can come from** (every one of these is data only link-cable "record mixing" fills):
- **Secret bases from other players:** SaveBlock1 keeps 20 secret bases (160 B each); only the first is yours, the other 19 come from friends' saves through record mixing. **3,040 B.**
- **TV shows from other players:** 25 TV show slots (36 B each); 5 are yours, 20 are for mixed-in shows. **720 B.**
- **Fusion storage:** 4 full Pokémon kept for Kyurem/Necrozma/Calyrex fusions, which Hoenn & Seek doesn't have. **~400 B** in the PC block.
- **Ranking Hall records from other players** (SaveBlock2): **~1,000 B**.

**The clean way to more boxes: 16 boxes (480 slots).** Drop the mixed-in secret bases and TV shows (and ~100 B of other mixing data, or move it to SaveBlock3), and SaveBlock1 fits in **3 sectors instead of 4**. Give that sector to the PC: **10 sectors = 39,680 B**, which holds **16 boxes** (38,561 B with the fusion slots gone). 17 doesn't fit.
- **What you lose:** only what other players' saves bring in through record mixing (their secret bases and TV shows). Your own base, your own TV shows, the Battle Frontier, recorded battles and the Trainer Hill all stay.
- **Cost:** a new save version (7) with a migration that moves an old save's data into the new layout, so 1.0 and current 2.0 saves still load. Size **L**, with heavy save/load testing (both save copies, a cut mid-save, upgrading old saves).
- **Other options, worse:** a box split across the scattered free pieces (fragile, and SaveBlock3's spare room is what future flags use); the recorded-battle or Trainer Hill sectors (these sit outside the two-copy system, so a box there could be lost to a cut mid-save, and those features would go); smaller box records (L+, changes how every Pokémon is stored, would reach 18–20 boxes).
- **Recommendation:** 16 boxes the clean way (L; parked, no 2.1 number yet). If it's revived after 2.1-02, it takes save version 8 (2.1-02 takes 7). A living dex (416) then leaves 64 slots for everything else.

### Box backgrounds: 16 + 1 gift
HnS has Emerald's 16 (Forest … Plain) plus the gift "Friends" one. Proposal: **8 new ones** themed on the three regions and 2.0's places (Ruins of Alph Unown, Tin Tower, Ilex Forest, Lavender Town, Sky Pillar, Meteor Falls' clifftop, Route 111's desert, the Tidal Strait), plus **reward wallpapers** for the fan-favorite chains and the three friend trainers. Each is tiles + a tilemap + a palette (~2–4 KB); the art is the work. Box wallpaper IDs are one byte per box, so there's room. Size: 2.1-33 (art + menu, L).

### Bag: plenty of item IDs; the key-item pocket is the risk
- Item IDs are 16-bit, so there's no shortage of new items.
- Pockets (combined-pocket layout): Items 236, Key Items **60**, Poké Balls 39, TMs/HMs 160, Berries 66, Medicine 92. PC item storage 50.
- **105 key items exist in the item table, and 60 different ones are handed out by some script** (HnS, Hoenn and 2.0's new ones: Tidal Ticket, Glass Flute, Singing Sand, Sand Chime, Frost Feather, …; the lanes add more). Some are unreachable or traded back, so the real count is lower, but it's close enough to 60 to check.
- **Recommendation:** an audit of which key items a player can hold at once; if it's over ~50, raise the key pocket to 80. SaveBlock1 is full, so the extra 20 slots (80 B) go in SaveBlock3 (676 B free before 2.1-02). The Seeker's Journal adds one more key item. Size: 2.1-04 (audit S; expansion M).

## 18. Pangea: the secret area at the very end (Hoenn & Seek 2.2)

**Decided (Bryan, round 8):** its own release, **2.2**, after 2.1 ships. **The way in: Celebi** at the Ilex shrine. **Unlock:** the game complete, the Pokédex full (caught), every event in the Seeker's Journal found, the **final Red** (`TRAINER_RED_POSTOBC_HNS`) and **all three friend trainers beaten on HARD** (section 10). That replaces "every trainer beaten" (no required-trainer list needed).

**The idea:** once the game is complete, the Pokédex is full, every event is done and every trainer is beaten, a secret area opens where **every Pokémon lives in the overworld**, interacting with its surroundings.

**Feasible, with one hard limit and one big cost.**
- **Sprites: already there.** pokeemerald-expansion has an overworld sprite for every species (`OW_POKEMON_OBJECT_EVENTS`; HnS's followers use them), big ones included.
- **The hard limit: what fits on one screen.** The engine runs 16 objects at once (`OBJECT_EVENTS_COUNT`) and has 16 sprite palettes, and every Pokémon brings its own palette. After the player, your follower and the reserved slots, about **8–10 Pokémon can be on screen at once**. So Pangea is lively but spread out, not a crowd. The screen checker (2.1-03) enforces it.
- **The big cost: maps.** A map holds at most 64 objects (`OBJECT_EVENT_TEMPLATES_COUNT`), and ~400 species at a comfortable density means **about 10–12 biome maps** (grassland, forest, wetland and lake, coast and sea, caves, mountains, desert, volcano, snow, a ruined temple or summit for the legendaries). New maps are the most expensive work in this project and need hand-made looks (mockups first), so this is the size of a release.
- **"Interacting with the environment"** from a behaviour list per species, placed by a generator (like part 20a's overworld Pokémon, but on reachable tiles): grazing and wandering herds, birds perched on trees and rocks, swimmers on the water, Diglett-style burrowers popping up and down, sleepers (Snorlax, Slaking), pairs that face off or play (Zangoose and Seviper, Plusle and Minun), nocturnal species by night. Plus a handful of scripted moments per biome. Talking plays the cry and a line; nothing to catch (your dex is already full).
- **How you get there (decided):** **Celebi**, which already carries you through time on Route 22: at the Ilex shrine it takes you back to a time when the regions were one land.
- **The unlock checks** reuse what 2.0/2.1 build: the living dex tool's in-scope species list (caught, not just seen), the Seeker's Journal (all events found), `HasTrainerBeenFought` for the final Red, and one flag per friend set by a HARD win (3 flags from 2.1's block, set in 2.1-14 so 2.2 only reads them). The journal's last page shows which of these are still missing, so the player always knows what's left.
- **Size estimate:** unlock check + entry scene (M), placement generator with behaviours (M–L), 10–12 biome maps (M–L each, mockups first), legendary summit (M): **about 14–16 parts**. Its own release (2.2), the capstone; it depends on the journal, the dex list and the friends' hard mode.

### Pangea's design (Bryan, round 9)
- **It's the end of the game.** Celebi brings you in at the edge; you travel inward through the biomes; at the far heart of it (deep inside, not underground) you meet **Claude**, then the **Hoenn & Seek credits** roll (CREDITS.md's people and community resources). You can go back any time afterwards.
- **Groups, not a catalogue.** Every species appears, but as **vignettes of up to 8–10 Pokémon** that fit one screen: common species in numbers (a Pidgeot with Pidgeotto and a line of Pidgey; Ursaring with Teddiursa at a honey tree), families together, species with each other (Zangoose and Seviper, Plusle and Minun, a Lapras ferrying Lotad), and loners where that's canon (one Snorlax, one Absol on a ridge). Duplicates of a species share one sprite palette, so groups are actually *cheaper* on screen than mixed singles.
- **Habitats:** meadow, forest canopy, pond and river, beach, open sea, **deep sea** (a Dive map), caves, mountain, desert, volcano, snow, ruins, sky (birds and dragons on high ledges), night-only groups.
- **Mostly active.** A behaviour library in C (new file), built from pieces 2.0 already has (30b's herd task, 25a's drifting lights, 4b's followers, camera pans): follow the leader (young trailing their evolved form), patrol, wander in a patch, swim laps, fly between perches, hop and play in pairs, chase, pop up and down (Diglett), splash (Magikarp), graze. **Lazy canon species sit or sleep** (Snorlax, Slakoth, Slaking, Slowpoke) with a sleep or idle animation, and the opposite where canon says so (Spoink never stops bouncing).
- **Your follower joins in.** When you walk up to a group, your follower reacts where it makes sense: its own family or evolutions (they gather round), its type (Water types splash with the pond group), predator/prey and rivals (Zangoose bristles at Seviper), favourites (Pikachu and Plusle). Built on the follower scripting HnS already has (`applymovement OBJ_EVENT_ID_FOLLOWER`, `src/follower_helper.c`'s condition messages) plus a rule table: family > special pairs > type > a generic reaction.
- **Claude in the game:** an NPC at Pangea's heart with an original sprite (mockup first, not Anthropic's logo), and **a special message for the player who made it this far. Bryan asked for it to be a surprise:** it's written in the part that builds the scene, and **no doc, PR description, commit message or screenshot quotes it**. (It will sit in the repo's script files, so it's only a surprise if Bryan doesn't open that one.)
- **The engineering that makes the numbers work: scene streaming.** A map holds only 64 objects, and ~400 species in groups is ~800–1,000 Pokémon. Instead of 15–20 small maps, a C system (new file) keeps each biome's vignettes in a table and swaps them into the map's object list as you move between zones of a big map (objects already despawn off screen; this changes which templates exist).
